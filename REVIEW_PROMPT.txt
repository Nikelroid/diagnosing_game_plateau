You are one of four experts reading this workshop paper for the second time. It goes to the

Round one found real defects. Every one was fixed, and one of them changed an argument in the

different hands. Heavy editing leaves seams: a paragraph at a different reading level, a term
that changed meaning halfway through, a claim narrowed in one place and left wide in another.
Those seams are what you are hunting.

PART TWO: CONSISTENCY AND READABILITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the part we most want from you, and it is the part an author cannot do for himself.

The house style, which the paper is trying to hold:

  Plain words. Say what happened, in the order it happened.
  Average sentence length between 16 and 18 words. It currently measures 16.0.
  Sentences of 20 to 25 words are welcome and give the writing rhythm. Almost nothing past 25.
  No sentence built from three or more ideas stacked with commas.
  No em dashes and no en dashes.
  No choppiness either. Six short sentences in a row is a defect, like one tangled sentence.

Report, in your slot, under a heading CONSISTENCY:

  A. TERMS THAT DRIFT. The paper defines support bits, worth, capture, banked, widening, the
     reference, the arms. Find any place where one of these is used loosely, renamed mid-paper,
     or used before it is defined. Quote both places.
  B. CLAIMS NARROWED IN ONE PLACE AND WIDE IN ANOTHER. The abstract, the section text, the
     captions, the appendix and the conclusion must all say the same size of thing. Captions are
     the usual offender because they get edited last. Quote the pair.
  C. THE TEN WORST SENTENCES. Rank them. For each, give the rewrite, not a description of what
     is wrong. Keep the meaning exactly; if your rewrite changes what the sentence claims, say
     so and do not use it.
  D. SEAMS. Any paragraph that reads as though a different person wrote it. Quote the first
     sentence and say what marks it as different.
  E. THE OPENING AND THE CLOSING. Read the abstract and the conclusion back to back. Do they
     promise and deliver the same paper? Quote any mismatch.

Then give one number: your estimate of the average sentence length, and the longest sentence in
the paper quoted in full.

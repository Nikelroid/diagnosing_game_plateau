**THE MISSING CLAIM**  
The placebo arm is not merely a null control; across the 88 runs it is the only quantity that is *inside seed noise in every game*, and that fact is what makes the Gin-Rummy null interpretable. The paper never states the stronger, data-supported sentence: “A wider observation costs the learner nothing measurable on any of the eleven solvable games.”

**THE PAPER I WOULD WRITE**  
Title: Support Bits, Worth, and Capture: Separating What a Game Hides from What a Learner Banks.  
One claim: observational ambiguity and the value of information are distinct quantities, and a learner’s plateau can be decomposed into the two.  
Three results: (1) atlas of 88 games showing two-player card games saturate while multi-player games do not; (2) oracle-observation null on Gin Rummy that survives a matched placebo; (3) exact decomposition on eleven solvable games in which worth is invariant to a 64-fold budget change while capture moves.  
This is essentially the paper the authors already have.  

**THE ONE EXPERIMENT**  
Re-run the Gin-Rummy oracle arm with a structured encoding of the opponent’s hand (suit counts + rank histogram + meld indicators) instead of the raw 10-card plane, twelve seeds, same recipe.  
If the structured arm still sits inside the baseline interval, the paper can claim “neither the raw channel nor a natural structured encoding of the same information moves the needle”; if it rises by >1.5 points the paper can claim “the information is usable once the representation matches the learner.” Cost: ~12 GPU-hours.  

**WHAT A HOSTILE REVIEWER SAYS FIRST**  
“The title asks whether it is the game or the agent, yet the only large game examined shows a null, the solvable games are all toy-sized, and capture exceeds 100 % in seven of them. The central diagnostic is therefore either inapplicable or biased on the very domains that matter.”  
Authors’ reply: “The null is deliberately narrow—this learner, this recipe, this reference—and the bias is reported with its sign and its perfect rank correlation inside each family; we never treat capture as an unbiased point estimate. The atlas and the placebo together already reorder the next experiments one would run on any larger domain.”  

**TITLE**  
The title promises a general diagnosis; the paper delivers a carefully scoped one. Narrower title that matches the evidence: “Support Bits and Oracle Observation: Measuring What Card Games Hide and Why One Agent Stops Improving.”

WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT
BRAINSTORM.txt has nine sections, S1 to S9. Each states something the authors believe, or a
problem they have, followed by the numbers behind it. Every number is read from result files

The work in one paragraph. The authors measure how much a game hides, in bits, across 88 game
configurations. Separately they ask why a reinforcement learning agent stops improving: they
hand the learner the hidden state and see whether it does better. On Gin Rummy it does not. They
now propose that a plateau decomposes into three measurable parts: what the information is
worth, what share the learner banks, and what the wider observation costs.

HOW TO ANSWER
Write into your slot under each section you have something to say about. Use this form:

  <VERDICT> | <what you would do, in one or two sentences> | <why it changes the paper>

Verdicts:
  KEEP        this is load-bearing; say what it is carrying
  CUT         this does not earn its space; say what the space should hold instead
  REFRAME     the finding is real but the framing is wrong; give the better framing
  DIG         there is more in this than the authors have taken; say what to measure
  DOUBT       you do not believe the claim; say what would convince you
  RISK        this is where the paper gets attacked; say by whom and how to defend it

Be concrete. "Strengthen the motivation" is not advice. "Cut S6 and use the space to run S9-E,
because the dial holds rules fixed while bits move and nothing else in the paper does" is advice.
